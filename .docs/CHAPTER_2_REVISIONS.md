### 2.8 Chapter Summary

This chapter has reviewed the academic and practical foundations underpinning the SmartFinance project across six areas: financial literacy among Malaysian young adults, behavioural economics and cognitive biases, gamification in financial technology, user experience design principles, mobile development technologies, and the Malaysian fintech landscape.

The reviewed literature confirms that Malaysian young adults aged 18–30 face documented financial literacy challenges that existing tools have failed to adequately address. Behavioural economics research establishes that psychological barriers — particularly present bias and loss aversion — require thoughtful design interventions beyond simple information provision, providing theoretical grounding for the gamification and nudge strategies adopted in SmartFinance. UX research from the Nielsen Norman Group and W3C provides practical guidelines for creating trustworthy, usable financial interfaces appropriate for the target demographic. The gap analysis of existing Malaysian fintech solutions confirms the absence of a product that combines accessible budgeting, basic investment tracking, and culturally appropriate behavioural design for financial education purposes.

Together, these findings establish that SmartFinance addresses a genuine and documented need, and that the design decisions outlined in subsequent chapters are grounded in established research rather than assumption. Chapter 3 details how these theoretical foundations are translated into specific requirements and system design decisions.
