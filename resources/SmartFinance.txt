SmartFinance: A Gamified Cross-Platform Mobile App for Personal Budgeting & Investment
Tracking for Malaysian Young Adults Abstract
Abstract
Financial literacy among Malaysian young adults remains critically low, with many lacking
adequate financial knowledge essential for long-term stability. This project addresses the gap
in culturally appropriate, engaging financial management tools by developing SmartFinance,
a gamified cross-platform mobile application specifically designed for Malaysian young
adults aged 18-30. The application combines practical financial management with behavioral
design principles to encourage sustainable financial habits through user-friendly expense
tracking, budget management, and basic investment portfolio monitoring.
The project employs Agile development methodology with user-centered design integration,
utilizing Flutter for cross-platform mobile development, Python Flask for backend services,
and MySQL for secure data management. The solution incorporates gamification elements
including achievement badges, habit streaks, and progress celebrations to address
psychological barriers that prevent consistent financial tracking among young adults. Core
functionality includes intelligent expense categorization, visual budget monitoring, spending
pattern analysis, and manual investment tracking without third-party banking integration to
ensure data privacy and accessibility.
User feedback is gathered through Google Forms surveys distributed to university students
and young professionals, with usability testing conducted among peers and fellow students to
validate the user experience design and feature effectiveness. The application targets
university students managing RM500-1500 monthly allowances and entry-level professionals
earning RM2000-4000 monthly, addressing their specific financial literacy development
needs.
The project contributes to Malaysian financial literacy improvement by providing culturally
sensitive financial tools that understand local banking habits, cash-based transactions, and
conservative investment approaches. Expected outcomes include improved user financial
consistency through tracked behavior patterns, enhanced financial literacy through guided
experiences, and sustainable habit formation through motivational design elements. The
solution demonstrates practical application of Human-Computer Interaction principles and
behavioral psychology in financial technology, serving as a reference framework for future
culturally appropriate fintech solutions in Southeast Asia while addressing the critical need
for accessible financial education tools among Malaysian emerging adults.

1 Introduction
This chapter presents an overview of the SmartFinance project, a gamified cross-platform
mobile application designed to address financial literacy challenges among Malaysian young
adults. The chapter outlines the project objectives, background context, proposed solution,
project scope, and organizational structure necessary to deliver a comprehensive personal
budgeting and investment tracking solution.
1.1 Project Background
Young adults in Malaysia face considerable challenges when it comes to managing personal
finances effectively. Despite widespread access to smartphones and digital technology, many
struggle to develop consistent financial management habits. This disconnect between
available technology and actual usage suggests that current financial management tools fail to
address the specific needs and behavioral patterns of this demographic.
Sustainable Development Goals (SDG) Alignment: This project directly supports SDG 4
(Quality Education) by promoting financial literacy education through accessible digital
tools. The application also contributes to SDG 8 (Decent Work and Economic Growth) by
empowering young adults with financial management capabilities essential for economic
participation and long-term financial stability. By providing educational financial tools, the
project addresses the critical gap in practical financial education that traditional academic
curricula often overlook.
1.1.1 Current Financial Management Challenges
The difficulty young adults face in maintaining consistent financial tracking habits stems
from a complex interplay of psychological and practical factors. Behavioral economics
research has shown that cognitive biases such as present bias — where immediate
gratification is valued over future benefits — and loss aversion significantly undermine
sustained financial management efforts (Kahneman & Tversky, 1979). Traditional financial
tools often overlook these behavioral principles, focusing instead on feature-rich interfaces
that can overwhelm users rather than motivate them.

Existing financial management applications present several limitations for Malaysian young

integrations that many students find cumbersome. Similarly, GrabPay provides basic expense
tracking without the motivational elements needed to maintain user engagement over time.
Traditional banking apps face similar challenges. Research indicates that a significant
proportion of young adults aged 18-25 find conventional banking apps overwhelming and
discontinue use within the first month (Asian Banking & Finance, 2023). The pattern that
emerges from these local solutions is clear: while the technical capabilities exist, user
experience design and behavioral engagement strategies fail to meet the specific needs of
Malaysian young adults who are transitioning from financial dependence to independence.
Summary of Key Challenges: The primary financial management challenges facing

● Cognitive biases that prioritize immediate gratification over long-term financial
planning
● Overwhelming complexity in existing financial applications that discourages
sustained usage
● Cultural mismatch between Western-designed tools and Malaysian financial practices
● Lack of motivational elements to encourage consistent tracking behaviors
● Insufficient educational support for users developing basic financial literacy
These interconnected challenges necessitate a solution specifically designed for Malaysian

1.1.2 Target Demographic Analysis
This project targets two distinct but related segments within Malaysia's young adult
population. The first consists of university students aged 18-24 who typically manage
monthly allowances ranging from RM500 to RM1,500. For many in this group, this
represents their first experience handling personal finances independently. Preliminary
discussions with university students suggest that while many attempt various financial
tracking methods, maintaining consistent usage remains a significant challenge.
The second segment comprises young professionals aged 22-30, typically entry-level workers
earning between RM2,000 and RM4,000 monthly. This group needs tools that strike a
balance between sophistication and accessibility, particularly for investment tracking and
long-term financial planning as they establish their careers and build financial foundations.
Rationale for Target Selection: These specific age groups and income ranges were selected
based on several factors. The 18-30 age range represents a critical transition period from
financial dependence to independence, where financial habits and attitudes are still forming
and malleable. Survey data from preliminary research indicated that this demographic
demonstrates both the highest need for financial literacy support and the greatest potential for
adopting digital solutions. The income ranges (RM500-1,500 for students and
RM2,000-4,000 for young professionals) represent typical Malaysian financial situations for
these groups, as documented by the Department of Statistics Malaysia (2024). These ranges
are sufficiently narrow to enable focused feature design while broad enough to accommodate
variations within each segment. Additionally, these users possess sufficient smartphone
proficiency to adopt mobile financial applications but often lack the financial sophistication
targeted by professional-grade financial software.

3.1 Development Methodology
3.1.1 Agile Development with User-Centered Design Integration
This project integrates Agile Development methodology with User-Centered Design (UCD)
principles. This combination ensures responsive development cycles and allows consistent
incorporation of user feedback throughout the development lifecycle. This hybrid approach
makes particular sense for financial application development, where several factors come into
play simultaneously.
The decision to use Agile rather than traditional Waterfall methodology stems from the
unique challenges of developing financial applications. Financial apps require continuous
validation of user trust — something that cannot be fully assessed until users actually interact
with the system. The iterative nature of Agile allows testing of trust-building elements early
and adjustment based on real user reactions. Additionally, given the constraints of a Final
Year Project timeline, Agile's flexibility helps accommodate technical challenges without
derailing the entire project schedule. If a particular feature proves more complex than
anticipated, priorities can be adjusted without compromising core functionality.
User-Centered Design principles are woven into each Agile iteration to ensure that user needs
remain central to development decisions. During sprint planning, user research activities are
conducted to understand what features matter most to the target demographic. Throughout
development sprints, usability testing is performed to catch issues early. Sprint retrospectives
then incorporate user feedback analysis, allowing refinement of approaches for the next
iteration. This integration means that technical implementation decisions are always validated
against user experience requirements rather than being made purely on technical grounds.

4.1.1 High-Level System Architecture
The system comprises three primary layers:

components optimized for smartphone screens.
Application Layer (Backend Server): Implemented using Python Flask, this layer processes
business logic including expense categorization algorithms, budget calculation engines,
investment performance analytics, and gamification reward systems. The application layer
exposes RESTful API endpoints that enable secure communication between mobile clients
and the database.
Data Layer (Database): MySQL database management system stores all persistent data
including user accounts, financial transactions, budget configurations, investment portfolios,
and achievement progress. The data layer implements security measures including encryption
for sensitive financial information and secure authentication mechanisms.

Justification for Three-Tier Architecture:
The three-tier architecture was chosen for several specific reasons aligned with project
objectives:
1. Cross-Platform Requirement: The project aims to deliver SmartFinance on both
Android and iOS platforms. A three-tier architecture allows a single Flask backend to
serve both mobile platforms simultaneously. Alternative monolithic approaches would
require developing separate backends for each platform, doubling development effort
and maintenance complexity.
2. Timeline Constraints: The Final Year Project operates within strict semester
timelines. The three-tier architecture provides appropriate complexity — sophisticated
enough to demonstrate technical competency while manageable within academic
constraints. Microservices architecture, while offering superior scalability, introduces
deployment complexity and distributed system challenges unsuitable for FYP
timelines.
3. Security Centralization: Financial applications require robust security measures.
The three-tier architecture centralizes authentication, authorization, and data
encryption at the application layer. This centralization simplifies security
implementation compared to distributed architectures where security must be
replicated across multiple services.
4. Scalability for Future Development: While the initial deployment targets limited
users (beta testing with 12-15 participants), the architecture supports future scaling.
The application layer can be deployed across multiple server instances behind a load
balancer without architectural redesign. This scalability potential makes the project
viable for post-FYP development.
5. Technology Stack Compatibility: Flutter's architecture naturally aligns with
three-tier patterns. Flutter applications function as presentation-layer clients that
communicate with backend services via HTTP APIs. This compatibility reduces
integration complexity compared to alternative frameworks requiring different
architectural approaches.
6. Development Resource Efficiency: As a single-developer FYP, resource efficiency
matters significantly. The three-tier architecture provides clear boundaries between

Justification for MySQL Selection:

assembly unsuitable for financial queries.

5. Performance for Application Scale: While the initial deployment targets limited

6. Academic and Industry Relevance: MySQL skills transfer directly to industry

4.3.3 Database Normalization and Structure Justification
The database design adheres to Third Normal Form (3NF) to eliminate data redundancy and
improve data integrity.
Normalization Benefits for SmartFinance:
1. Elimination of Update Anomalies
Without normalization, updating a user's email address might require modifying multiple
transaction records. With normalized structure, email updates occur once in the USERS table,
automatically reflecting across all associated records through foreign key relationships.
Example: If user profiles were denormalized (user data duplicated in each transaction
record), changing an email address would require updating potentially hundreds of
transaction records. This approach introduces consistency risks where some transactions
show old emails while others show new emails. Normalization eliminates this risk entirely.
2. Data Integrity Through Constraints
Foreign key constraints enforce referential integrity automatically:
FOREIGN KEY (UserId) REFERENCES USERS(UserId) ON DELETE CASCADE
This constraint prevents invalid data insertions. For example, attempting to create a
transaction for a non-existent user ID fails at the database level, preventing application bugs
from corrupting data.
Justification: Database-level constraints provide defense-in-depth security. Even if
application code contains bugs that attempt invalid operations, the database rejects
inconsistent data, maintaining integrity.
3. Storage Efficiency
Normalized structures reduce storage requirements through data sharing. For example,
achievement definitions (name, description, badge icon) exist once in ACHIEVEMENTS
table but relate to thousands of user unlocks through the bridge table. Denormalized storage
would duplicate achievement descriptions for each user unlock, wasting space.

4.4.1 Architectural Pattern Selection
SmartFinance implements a Three-Tier Client-Server Architecture pattern, chosen through
systematic evaluation of architectural alternatives against project requirements.
Selected Architecture: Three-Tier Client-Server
The three-tier architecture separates the system into three distinct layers:
1. Presentation Layer: Mobile client (Flutter)
2. Application Layer: Backend server (Flask)
3. Data Layer: Database server (MySQL)
Advantages of Three-Tier Architecture:
● Separation of Concerns: Clear boundaries between user interface, business logic,
and data management facilitate parallel development and testing
● Independent Scalability: Each layer can be scaled independently based on
performance bottlenecks
● Centralized Security: Authentication, authorization, and data encryption are
implemented once at the application layer
● Cross-Platform Support: Single backend serves multiple client platforms (Android,
iOS)
● Maintainability: Changes to one layer minimally impact other layers, reducing
regression risks

The SmartFinance system implements a three-tier client-server architecture separating

monolithic and microservices alternatives based on project requirements for cross-platform
compatibility, manageable complexity, and centralized security. The architecture supports

each layer.

The UI design prioritizes trust-building, cultural appropriateness, and motivational elements
essential for financial application adoption among Malaysian young adults. Screen designs
for authentication, dashboard, expense entry, budget monitoring, investment tracking, and
achievement display incorporate established UX principles including progressive disclosure,
error prevention, and recognition over recall. The design applies Malaysian cultural context
through localized terminology (mamak, Grab) and category structures reflecting regional
spending patterns. Navigation architecture combines persistent bottom navigation for primary
sections with hierarchical back-button navigation for detailed screens, balancing accessibility
with complexity management.
Data Architecture (Section 4.3):
The database design implements normalized relational structure reducing data redundancy
while maintaining referential integrity through foreign key constraints. Eight core tables
(USERS, TRANSACTIONS, BUDGETS, BUDGETCATEGORIES, INVESTMENTS,

with clearly defined relationships. The schema employs strategic denormalization for
performance optimization (cached SpentAmount in BUDGETCATEGORIES) while
maintaining data accuracy through application-level consistency management. MySQL was
selected as the database management system providing ACID compliance essential for
financial data integrity, mature ecosystem support, and Python integration efficiency.
